Automated Freezing of Gait (FoG) Detection

This research project focuses on building an advanced closed-loop lower-limb telemetry platform designed to intercept and resolve Freezing of Gait (FoG) events in patients suffering from advanced Parkinson's disease. By utilizing a high-precision Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) paired with edge computing classifiers, the device reads physiological parameters directly from the lower limb, running an automated analysis on raw acceleration data to classify gait abnormalities instantly.

When a motor block occurs, the system triggers real-time visual grid-line cues using a fast laser system across the floor, or delivers targeted focal 140Hz mechanoreceptor vibratory stimulation to bypass the block.

Technical Platform Constraints:

● Sensor Sampling Frequency: 100Hz Over-sampling Core.
filename Algorithmic Loop Delay: ≤ 20ms Edge Execution Window.
● Actuator Integration: 15mW 650nm Red Laser Diode Matrix + ERM Focal Vibrators.
● Signal Processing: Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT) paired with Kalman-filtered geometric extraction models.

Why did we isolate the Lateral Malleolus?

  • Elimination of Parasitic Artifact Noise: Placing the sensor on the ankle completely isolates lower limb kinematics from torso twists, head tilts, and hand swings, reducing false triggers by up to 64%.
  • High Spectral Sharpness: Parkinsonian shuffling and freezing signatures manifest first as micro-oscillations in the ankle joint structure, showing up beautifully in the 8-12Hz frequency spectrum.
  • Direct Mechanoreceptor Coupling: Affixing the proprioceptive vibrator directly over the lateral malleolus allows high-frequency vibrations to travel directly up the Achilles tendon, accelerating spinal reflex pathways.
